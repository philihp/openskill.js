# `weight` and `weightBounds`

`weight` scales each player's update by how much of the game they played.
Default: no weights. `weightBounds` is the range that the weights of a team
are normalized into. Default: `[1, 2]`. Both apply to every model.

## What they do

`weight` has the same shape as `teams`: one number per player. After `rate`
computes a team's update, it multiplies each player's change in `mu`, and
the shrink of `sigma`, by that player's weight. On a losing team, it divides
by the weight instead. So on a winning team, a larger weight means a larger
gain, and on a losing team, a larger weight means a smaller loss.

Before that, with `weightBounds`, `rate` rescales the weights of each team.
The smallest weight becomes the lower bound and the largest becomes the
upper bound. Only the order and the spacing within a team matter, not the
raw numbers. With two players, the weights always become `[1, 2]` or
`[2, 1]`, whatever you pass. Equal weights all become the lower bound. With
`weightBounds: null`, `rate` uses the raw weights.

## When to change them

- Use `weight` for time played, or for a measure of contribution.
- Keep the default bounds when your weights are on an arbitrary scale.
- Set `weightBounds: null` when the weights have a meaning of their own, such
  as the fraction of the game played. Then keep them near 1. A weight of 2
  doubles a gain.

```js
rate(teams, {
  weight: [
    [1, 0.5], // the second player played half the game
    [1, 1],
  ],
  weightBounds: null,
})
```
