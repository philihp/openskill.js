# `margin`

`margin` makes the size of a win matter. Default: none. It needs `score`,
and it applies to every model. It gives the same results as `margin` in
openskill.py.

## What it does

For each pair of teams, the model finds the score difference. If the
difference is more than `margin`, the model computes a divisor:
`log1p(difference / margin)`. If the difference is not more than `margin`,
the divisor is 1.

- The Bradley-Terry and Thurstone-Mosteller models divide the difference in
  `mu` between the two teams by the divisor. Then they compute the expected
  result from this smaller or larger difference.
- Plackett-Luce moves the `mu` of each team toward or away from the `mu` of
  each opponent with a different score, by `divisor − 1` times the gap. It
  uses the mean of these moves.

Thus `margin` has an effect only when the teams have different `mu`:

- Two teams with the same `mu` get the same update with or without
  `margin`.
- When the score difference is more than about 1.72 × `margin`, the divisor
  is more than 1. The gap in `mu` looks smaller, so a favorite that wins
  gets more `mu`, and an underdog that wins gets less.
- When the score difference is between `margin` and about 1.72 × `margin`,
  the divisor is less than 1. The gap in `mu` looks larger, and the effect
  is the opposite.
- The divisor grows with the logarithm of the difference, so a win by 40
  points does not count as twice a win by 20.

## When to change it

- Set `margin` to the score difference that separates a close game from a
  clear one, in the points of your game.
- `margin` must be more than 0. With 0, or without `score`, nothing changes.
- Give one score for each team. If the number of scores is not the same as
  the number of teams, the models ignore the margin.

```js
rate(teams, { score: [37, 12], margin: 10 })
```
